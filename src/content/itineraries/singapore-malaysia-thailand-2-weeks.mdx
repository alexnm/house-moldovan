## Days 1-3: Singapore

After a long flight from Istanbul you would expect to be exhausted, but Singapore is the kind of city that will energize you, even on a hot humid day at 30°C. s we later found out, that's just called a day in Singapore.

<AssetFigure
  path="singapore/gardens-by-the-bay-day.JPG"
  alt="Gardens by the Bay during the day."
  caption="Gardens by the Bay during the day."
/>

We instantly felt in love with the city, with its green spaces and clean streets. The promenade along the Singapore river is one of the best spots to enjoy evening strolls or some running.

And of course the famous Marina Bay area is absolutely breathtaking day and night.

<AssetFigure
  path="singapore/marina-bay-sands.JPG"
  alt="Marina Bay Sands at night."
  caption="Marina Bay Sands at night."
/>

Don't miss the Gardens by the Bay evening shows and the Spectra light show in front of Marina Bay Sands. It's hard to describe how captivating these shows are.

What's unique about Singapore is the mix of cultures that can be sampled in the historical neighbourhoods: Chinatown, Little India and Kampong Glam, the latter being the muslim quarter.

<AssetFigure
  path="singapore/arab-street.JPG"
  alt="Arab Street in Singapore."
  caption="Arab Street in Singapore."
/>

There are tons of stereotypes about Singapore's strict laws, and while some of those are true (like a strict ban on any kind of drugs), the reality is that the city is one of the safest and most welcoming places in the world.

One thing which is definitely true is that the city is very expensive. Hotels, restaurants and bars tend to be 50% more expensive than even the most expensive cities in Europe. Luckily, you can enjoy the numerous hawker centers around the city, which are a great way to sample the local streetfood culture.

<AssetFigure
  path="singapore/chinatown.JPG"
  alt="Chinatown in Singapore."
  caption="Chinatown in Singapore."
/>

For our second and third day in Singapore we booked tours with [Monster Day Tours](https://www.monsterdaytours.com/category/singapore-free-walking-tours). They offer a lot of free walking tours of the historical neighbourhoods and they have great local guides. We did the Chinatown tour and Little India and when we later returned to Singapore at the end of the trip, we did the generic City Tour. All three of these were great and we learned a lot about the city and its history.

## Days 4-9: Kuala Lumpur

While Kuala Lumpur shares a lot of similar with Singapore, it is definitely more affordable and less crowded, but it is also a bit more chaotic. It has that stereotypical modern Asian city feel, with busy markets and huge luxurious shopping malls. It also is less people oriented and more car centric, making it a bit more challenging to navigate around.

The city is also known for having some of the most affordable 5-star hotels in the world. For accommodation we picked an apartment in the Eaton Residence building, which turned out to be a great choice. The apartment itself was a regular apartment fully equiped, but the building has one of the best rooftop pools in the city, completely free of charge for all tenants.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/kl-rooftop-pool.JPG"
  alt="The view from the 52nd floor was breathtaking."
  caption="The view from the 52nd floor was breathtaking."
/>

Malaysia is a very interesting country. It is a muslim majority coutnr, but it doesn't feel extremely religious or conservative. Locals are very friendly and welcoming and very open to interact with foreigners.

It is also not a very touristic country, especially compared to Vietnam, Thailand or other similar countries around. This makes it harder to find good day tours and activities, but it also makes the whole experience more authentic.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/kl-skyline.JPG"
  alt="The skyline of Kuala Lumpur in the evening."
  caption="The skyline of Kuala Lumpur in the evening."
/>

Similarly with Singpore, KL has a ton of foodcourts and streetfood areas and the food in Malaysia is some of the best in the world, as far as we are concerned. The blend of sweet salty and spicy flavours if out of this world and the streetfood is fresh and delicious.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/kl-jalan-alor.JPG"
  alt="Jalan Alor street in Kuala Lumpur."
  caption="Jalan Alor street in Kuala Lumpur."
/>

Don't miss the Jalan Alor street with its endless variety of terraces and food stalls. Some people might be skeptical about the cleanliness of the streetfood, but we found it to be very clean and the food was fresh and delicious.

<Info type="tip">
  The Jalan Alor street is well connected to the KLCC and the Petronas Towers
  via a long pedestrian bridge that bypasses several streets and has multipled
  entries and exits along the way.
</Info>

While in KL, make sure you visit the world-famous Petronas Towers, the tallest buildings in the world at the time of their construction. The KL Bird Park was also a great experience, as it is home to a huge variety of birds and animals.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/kl-petronas-towers.JPG"
  alt="The Petronas Towers at night."
  caption="The Petronas Towers at night."
/>

While staying in KL for a few days, we booked several day trips to explore the entire Malay peninsula.

### Cameron Highlands

The Cameron Highlands are famous for its tea plantations and mild temperatures, compared to the low lands of Malaysia. Our tour stopped at several locations including a strawberry farm, a butterfly farm where we saw some of the biggest butterflies in the world and, of course, the famous tea plantations.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/cameron-highlands.JPG"
  alt="The tea plantations in the Cameron Highlands."
  caption="The tea plantations in the Cameron Highlands."
/>

### Taman Negara

The Taman Negara is the oldest rainforest in the world and the largest national park in Malaysia. Funny enough, Taman Negara translates to "National Park" in Malay, so calling it Taman Negara National Park, as it's quite common in travel guides, is a bit weird for locals.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/taman-negara-river.JPG"
  alt="Cruising up and down the river in the Taman Negara."
  caption="Cruising up and down the river in the Taman Negara."
/>

We booked a tour for the entire day which turned out to be a private tour just driving us to the park and then having a local guide from the park while we were there. This is the part of Malaysia that we mentioned was rather underdeveloped for tourism, as you would usually expect a smaller group of travellers on these day tours.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/taman-negara-canopy.JPG"
  alt="The canopy walk in the Taman Negara."
  caption="The canopy walk in the Taman Negara."
/>

Some of the highlights of the park were the canopy walks where you get to experience the jungle from above as well as the cruise up and down the river. We also visited a local tribe and got to see how they live in the jungle.

The downside with these day trips from KL is that you spend quite some time in the car/bus, so keep that in mind when booking the tours.

### Batu Caves

One of the most impressive sights in Kuala Lumpur also requires some planning as it's quite far from the city center. We reached **Batu Caves** with a hired local driver and we spent a few hours there.

The caves are a natural limestone formation that host a hindu temple.The iconic colorful stairs leading to the cave system is also adorned with a giant statue of Lord Murugan, the god of war and destruction in hinduism.

Beware of the monkeys in the caves, they are quite aggressive and they are not afraid of tourists. We heard that they are particularly fond of sunglasses and hats, so keep an eye on your belongings while visintg the caves.

The same driver then took us to the **National Mosque of Malaysia** and to the **Then Hou Temple**, so they day was quite packed with visits to religious sites.

The caves are really worth a visit, the mosque is nice if you are interested in islamic architecture and the temple is nice and colorful, but if you are tight with time you can also skip it.

## Days 10-11: Penang

Our initial plan was to take a train from KL to Penang, but upon doing more research, we decided to fly with a low cost company as it was both cheaper and faster to reach the north of the country.

Penang is the second largest city in Malaysia but it contrasts the modern look of KL with the colonial buildings and old narrow streets of Georgetown.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/penang-street.JPG"
  alt="The colonial buildings in Georgetown."
  caption="The colonial buildings in Georgetown."
/>

Fun fact, Penang is actually a state, not a city, but Malaysia has a weird way of organizing cities and states. As a tourist, you will usually refer to Penang as the city, but the old colonial part is called Georgetown. The old town is actually situated on an island just off the coast of the mainland, while Penang state is the island plus a chunk of the mainland. It's confusing, we know...

### Georgetown

The old part of Penang is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and it is home to thousands of traditional shop houses built during the colonial era in the early 18th century.

It is also home to the Clan Jetties, which are a group of traditional Chinese fishing villages built on water that are still in use today by the same families of Chinese immigrants that built them in the 19th century.

### Penang Hill

A quick taxi ride away from Georgetown is **Penang Hill**, which offers a panoramic view of the city and the sea. The hill is also designated as a nature reserve and has a lot of trails for nature walks.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/penang-hill.JPG"
  alt="Mela on a metal bridge on Penang Hill."
  caption="Walking around Penang Hill."
/>

You can hike up the hill or you can take the funicular railway from the Penang Hill station. Keep in mind that the funicular is quite busy during the weekends, as we found out during our visit.

### Kek Lok Si Temple

At the base of Penang Hill, you can visit the **Kek Lok Si Temple**, which is one of the largest buddhist temples in Southeast Asia. The temple is known for its towering pagoda and its golden Buddha statue.

<AssetFigure
  path="malaysia/kek-lok-si.JPG"

To travel between Georgetown, Penang Hill and the Kek Lok Si Temple, we used Grab, the local Uber alternative in Southeast Asia. It's reliable, fast and relatively cheap as distances aren't too long.

During the last day in Penang, we visited the Upside Down House, a popular tourist attraction with some very cool interior arrangements that create some fun illusions.

## Days 11-14: Phuket

Our 3rd country in the trip was Thailand. We looked for a place that is easy to reach from Penang and has beautiful beaches and crystal clear waters. While Malaysia's Langkawi is usually a popular destination after Penang, we decided to go to Phuket instead.

<AssetFigure
  path="thailand/patong-beach.JPG"
  alt="Sandy beach with palm trees in Patong."
  caption="Patong Beach during the day."
/>

Being for 3 days in Phuket does not do Thailand justice, but since we were so close and since Phuket is such a popular destination, we decided to give it a try but keep our expectations low.

Truthfully, the island is great, but it is also very touristy and packed with people. Airport transfers are long and the distance between the different towns makes it difficult to venture far from your resort without booking a tour or renting a car or scooters.

### Patong Beach

We decided to stay in Patong Beach, probably the most popular spot on the island. Patong is probably the hotspot of Phuket, with a long sandy beached and a vibrant nightlife.

<AssetFigure
  path="thailand/patong-sunset.JPG"
  alt="Sandy beach with palm trees in Patong at night."
  caption="Patong Beach at night."
/>

We're not really into drinking and partying while travelling, but we like to wander around during the evenings and enjoy the vibe of the place. Patong Beach and especially Bangla Road are the places to be if you want to experience the nightlife of Phuket.

### Phi Phi Islands

During one of the days in Phuket, we booked a tour with one of the many local tour operators to visit the famous islands of the Phi Phi archipelago that are known for their beautiful beaches and crystal clear waters.

<AssetFigure
  path="thailand/pileh-lagoon-2.JPG"
  alt="Crystal clear waters in a limestone bay at the Pileh Lagoon."
  caption="Crystal clear waters in a limestone bay at the Pileh Lagoon."
/>

The tour had multiple stops, including several stops including Maya Bay and Pileh Lagoon in Ko Phi Phi Lee, which are probably the most beautiful spots in the archipelago.

Maya Bay is also famous for being the location of the movie "The Beach" starring Leonardo DiCaprio. It used to be a public beach, but due to overcrowding and pollution, swimming is now forbidden.

<AssetFigure
  path="thailand/maya-bay.JPG"
  alt="Maya Bay in Ko Phi Phi Lee."
  caption="Maya Bay in Ko Phi Phi Lee."
/>

While we are aware these spots still suffer today from overtourism, we still recommend visiting. The scenery really is breathtaking and you can definitely understand why everyone wants to see it.

We also stopped for lunch or swim at several spots on Ko Phi Phi Don, which is the largest island in the archipelago. One of the spots was perfect for snorkeling. Finally we stopped on the Bamboo Island for an afternoon swim.

<Info type="warning">
  We recommend booking the tour at the official company booths spread around the
  island. All companies offer almost the same tours at roughly the same price.
  Hotels and street vendors will always add a commission to the tour price.
</Info>

## Days 13-14: Singapore

Before heading back home, we had two more evenings and one full day in Singapore to relax and enjoy the city without any plans. This was probably the moment when the city grew on us the most.

We went running in the evening at 11pm at 35°C and hummidity close to 100%. We visited the Art Science Museum and we stayed for numerous light shows at Gardens by the Bay and Marina Bay Sands.

<AssetFigure
  path="singapore/marina-bay-2.JPG"
  alt="The view of the skyline of Singapore at night from Marina bay."
  caption="Marina bay at night."
/>

It really felt like a proper travel experience, no pre-booked plans, no rush to see everything, just enjoying the city and the vibes.

We wandered around different neighbourhoods and for the afternoon we booked our final tour with Monster Day Tours. This covered the downtown area and ended on the rooftop of the old **Parliament House** building, a great spot to say goodbye to this amazing trip.
