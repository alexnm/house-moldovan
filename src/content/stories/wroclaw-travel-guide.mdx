## Introduction

Wrocław is usually eclipsed by Krakow, the bigger and more known city in the southern part of the country. However, we found the capital of [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia) to be the perfect spot for getting to know the Polish culture better.

The city is located on the Oder river, 3 hours drive away from Krakow. This makes it somewhat accessible, but still feels like a hidden gem. Most tours in Poland include Krakow and Warsaw, but they skip Wrocław.

Historically, this city has been part of the Prussian Empire and later the German Empire, so the architecture is distinctly german. However, the culture and vibe of the city is 100% polish, with the german influence being more of a backdrop than the main character.

The main square of Wrocław is one of the most beautiful in the country, with impressive buildings and a great atmosphere around the many bars and restaurants that open up during the day and evening.

## What to do in Wrocław

You can start your visit from the old market square, surrounded by colorful houses. The Old Town Hall is a beautiful example of gothic architecture and the nearby spire of St. Elizabeth's Church offers a great panoramic view of the entire city.

<AssetFigure

A bit further away from the heart of the city, you will find various small islands on the Oder river. On one of them you can visit the Wrocław Cathedral, rebuilt after it was completely destroyed during the Second World War.

One of the main activities while in Wrocław is [hunting dwarfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw%27s_dwarfs). There are over 300 small figurines spread around the city in some of the most popular places. Even if you are not looking for them, you will surely find some, in the most unexpected places.

<AssetFigure
  path="poland/wroclaw-dwarfs.jpg"

We spent an evening and a full day here back in 2016 during a very hot June, as we were touring Poland and the Baltic states. It was enough to see the main spots and enjoy the relaxed vibe of the city.
