Spain's highest mountain sits in the middle of Tenerife in the Canary Islands. Mount Teide (3,718m) is a volcanic cone rising from a high caldera that feels on a different planet compared to the sandy beaches of Tenerife.

We timed several of our visits during our two week stay in Tenerife for late afternoon. There are three locations that offer great sunset views with easy access from the TF-21 road that traverses the caldera.

Very close to the volcano itself, you can find the **Minas de San José**, a lunar landscape with several balconies that offer a wide view of the caldera. A bit farther to the south, you can stop by the **Roques de García**, a series of jagged volcanic peaks in the middle of a flat volcanic plain. This place has a direct open view towards the main peak of Teide.

Finally, as you descend from the caldera towards the south-west part of the island, you can stop at a pine forest near **Samara Peak**, where you can hike for 10-15 minutes to get a great viewpoint of the twin peaks of Teide and Pico Viejo turning orange-red as the sun sets behind you.
