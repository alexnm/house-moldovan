## About the trek

Everyone will have **Machu Picchu** on their bucket list, but what if you can experience it in a more adventurous way with a 4-day trek through the high Andes?

And no, this does not have anything to do with the more popular/famous **Inca Trail**, that crosses the Andean jungle and also ends in Machu Picchu.

The Salkantay Trek is a high altitude multi-day trek that takes you through some of the most imposing mountains peaks of Peru, then takes you down through the high jungle valleys to Machu Picchu.

It can trekked independently, or part of a package tour where your luggage is transported site to site and your food is provided by the tour company.

We went with the 4-day tour from [Salkantay Trekking](https://www.salkantaytrekking.com/) and it was one of the best decisions as we ended up with a small group of people with two guides and we had a amazing experience. Our guides Saul and Nico really made the whole trip special.

Keep in mind that a 4-day trip means 3 days of hiking and one day in Machu Picchu. Most tour companies have a few different options for the trek, but the 4/5 days trip is the most popular one.

## Day 1: Soraypampa and Humantay Lake

The proper trek starts west of Cusco, at the base of the mountains in Challacancha. The first day starts relatively easy and our guides took advantage of that to give us time to know each other better and interact with everyone else in the group.

After a few relatively easy kilometers you reach the camp at Soraypampa. At this point you are already at 3,800 meters above sea level, so starting slower is a good idea.

<AssetFigure
  path="peru/salkantay-iglu.JPG"
  alt="Iglu camp at Soraypampa"
  caption="Iglu camp at Soraypampa"
/>

One thing to mention is that all these group organized hikes include all the meals cooked by a chef at each campsite. And don't think for a second that the food is going to be bad. It was amazing, maybe even the best we had in Peru.

The company goes to great lengths to ensure the entire experience is top notch. They also provide plenty of snacks and drinks during the trek, so you can pretty much come empty handed, just carying your day backpack.

After having lunch at Soraypampa, there's a short but steep hike up to the Humantay Lagoon, a beautiful blue glacier lake at the foot of the mountain and glaciar with the same name.

<AssetFigure
  path="peru/salkantay-humantay.JPG"
  alt="Humantay Lagoon at the foot of the mountain"
  caption="Humantay Lagoon at the foot of the mountain"
/>

According to our guides, they use this short hike to figure out if people are in good shape for the rest of the trek. It's almost like a fitness test for day 2.

Back to Soraypampa for dinner and a good night's sleep. The campsite is located in a valley and has direct views towards the Salkantay peak. We were fortunate to have a clear morning on the second day and waking up for sunrise really was a treat.

## Day 2: Salkantay Pass

This is by far the most challenging day of the trip. Not only your reach the highest point of the trek, but you also hike the most distance, covering over 23 kilometers.

It starts early in the morning with an easier stretch first. But it quickly becomes a steep ascent to the Salkantay Pass. The pass is the highest point of the trek at 4,630 meters above sea level.

<AssetFigure
  path="peru/salkantay-trek.JPG"
  alt="The mountain in the distance as the hiking trail ascends towards the Salkantay Pass"
  caption="The mountain in the distance"
/>

Here's the thing. Everyone reacts differently to high altitude. Some people say they notice a different feel after 2000 meters, some say it happens at 3000. We were used to hiking at 2000 meters, but 4000 is a different beast.

Luckily you receive plenty of coca leaves tea or even dry leaves to chew in the morning, which really helps with the high altitude.

The ascent includes a famous narrow twisty section called the "Seven Snakes". The challenging part is not the terrain difficulty, but the constant stream of mules passing by, sometimes blocking the trail, other times going really fast downhill.

<AssetFigure
  path="peru/salkantay-seven-snakes.JPG"
  alt="The seven snakes section of the trail"
  caption="The seven snakes section of the trail"
/>

Somehow, the seven snakes section went by really fast. But you can tell that it gets harder and harder to breathe and especially to relax at over 4000m. Our group moved pretty fast and we were right on schedule.

For the last part of the climb, everyone went at their own pace. We started feeling tired, but the scenery is so amazing that you have plenty of opportunities to take a break, take a picture and enjoy the view.
