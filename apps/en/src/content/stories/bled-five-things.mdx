## The postcard

Bled is the picture everyone already has of Slovenia: a church on an island, a castle on a cliff, and the Julian Alps behind them. The lake is small enough to walk around in an afternoon, and the town sits right on the edge of Triglav National Park, so the bigger mountains are close.

We stopped here on the way through the country a few years ago, long enough for the castle, the shore, and the view from above. It looks exactly like the photos. If you have two or three days, these are the five things worth your time.

## Be a tourist

Start with the obvious. The castle, the lake, and a boat to the island are famous because they are the reason to come.

**Bled Castle** sits on the cliff above the north shore. The walk up from town is short and steep. From the terrace you look straight down at the island and across to the mountains. The rooms inside cover the history of the town. The view from the walls is why you climb up.

**Bled Island** is a few minutes across the water. The traditional way over is a pletna, a wooden boat rowed by a local boatsman from the western shore. You can also rent a rowboat and go at your own pace. On the island, 99 steps lead up to the Church of the Assumption. Inside, people ring the bell and make a wish.

The promenade along the eastern shore is part of the same circuit: swans, rowboats, and the full view back toward the church and the castle together.

## Circle the lake

The path around the lake is about 6 kilometers and almost flat. An hour and a half if you keep moving, longer once you start stopping for the view.

The western side, under the castle cliff, is the quieter stretch of trees and water. The eastern side passes the town, the boats, and the open views toward the island. Morning is the calmest time on the path. Late in the day the mountains catch the light and the water goes still.

## Hike up to the viewpoint

The photo of Bled you have already seen is taken from above the south-west shore, at Mala Osojnica. The trail starts near the camping ground at the western end of the lake. It is short and steep, through forest and then over rock. Most people are up in about half an hour.

From the top you get the whole lake in one frame: the island, the castle, the town, and the Alps. A path continues to Velika Osojnica, the higher summit next door, if you want to keep walking. Ojstrica, on a fork lower down, is the easier viewpoint over the same water.

## Vintgar Gorge

Vintgar is a short trip north of town, cut by the Radovna river. A wooden boardwalk is fixed to the wall of the gorge, just above green water that moves fast. The walk ends at the Šum waterfall, under the stone arches of the railway bridge.

Summer afternoons get crowded, and the gorge is narrow, so the walkway fills up. Go when it opens. The boardwalk is seasonal, roughly late spring through autumn, and it closes for the winter.

## Hiking in the Julian Alps

Bled is the easy door into the range. For a first mountain day, drive twenty minutes west to Lake Bohinj. The Vogel cable car lifts you onto a ridge of meadows, with Triglav and the Bohinj valley in front of you. The walking stays gentle near the station and gets wilder as soon as you leave it.

<AssetFigure

For full days on the trails, Kranjska Gora is the better base. It sits deeper in the Alps, close to the Vršič Pass and the Soča valley. That is where we stayed when we came back to hike. Bled is the right place to start. The mountains are the reason to stay in the area longer.
