For centuries, the cities of Uzbekistan were the beating heart of the Silk Road. Today, the blue domes, tiled madrasas and busy bazaars of Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva make it one of the most fascinating destinations in Central Asia, in a country that only recently opened up to foreign travelers.

One of the most interesting countries we visited, Uzbekistan still lives in the shadow of its past glory. The cities here used to be the most important stops on the famous Silk Road.

But the country was for almost a century under Soviet rule and then it was closed to foreigners until the late 2010s. Today, it is fast becoming one of the most popular destinations in Central Asia and a must-see for anyone interested in the history of the Silk Road.

We visited Uzbekistan in 2019 on an eight day trip [following the Silk Road](/itineraries/uzbekistan-silk-road), from Tashkent to the walled city of **Khiva**, then on to **Bukhara**, Shahrisabz and finally **Samarkand**. The glaze we kept walking up to — Kalta Minor, Mir-i-Arab and Shah-i-Zinda — is in our [tilework spotlight](/spotlights/uzbekistan-tilework).
