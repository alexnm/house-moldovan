## Day 1: Amsterdam

Arrive at **Amsterdam Centraal** and drop your bags. Day one is for getting oriented: a canal-ring walk, the Nine Streets, and whatever museum you've booked ahead — Rijksmuseum or Van Gogh are the obvious picks. The city is flat, compact, and best explored on foot or by tram; save energy for an evening along the Amstel or in de Pijp.

## Day 2: Haarlem and Zaanse Schans

Take the train to **Haarlem** (15 minutes) for a quieter Dutch city experience — Grote Markt, the Frans Hals Museum if you have time, and the kind of café-lined streets Amsterdam used to have more of. In the afternoon, continue to **Zaanse Schans**, the open-air windmill village north of the city. It's touristy, but the working mills and green timber houses still deliver the postcard.

## Day 3: Utrecht, evening in Amsterdam

**Utrecht** is underrated. The Oudegracht is a two-level canal — cafés at water level, shops and streets above — and the Domtoren climbs above everything. It's 30 minutes by train from Amsterdam; spend the day here and come back for a final Amsterdam evening. If you're traveling in April, King's Day turns the city orange and chaotic in the best way.

## Day 4: Keukenhof, then Delft

Check out of Amsterdam and head south. In tulip season, stop at **Keukenhof** near **Lisse** — the world's largest flower garden, open roughly eight weeks each spring. Outside that window, swap this leg for Leiden or The Hague, both on the same corridor. Continue to **Delft** for the night: blue-and-white pottery, a perfectly scaled old town, and far fewer crowds than Amsterdam.

## Day 5: Delft and Rotterdam

Keep the morning for **Delft** — Vermeer's city rewards a slow walk before the tour groups arrive. In the afternoon, take the train to **Rotterdam** (15 minutes) for the opposite of Delft: bold architecture, the Markthal, Erasmus Bridge, and a harbor city rebuilt after the war. One base, two completely different moods.

## Day 6: Antwerp en route to Ghent

Train south into Belgium. **Antwerp** deserves a half-day even if you're not staying — the cathedral, the Grote Markt, and the fashion district around Nationalestraat. Then continue to **Ghent** (30 minutes). We prefer Ghent as a base over Bruges: it's a real city with medieval bones, a strong food scene, and far fewer coach parties.

## Day 7: Ghent

A full day with nowhere else to be. Walk the **Graslei** and **Korenlei**, climb **Gravensteen** if you want the view, and leave time for a long lunch. Ghent doesn't need a checklist — it's the kind of place where sitting by the canal with a beer is the activity.

## Day 8: Bruges

Finish with a day trip to **Bruges**. The train from Ghent takes about 25 minutes. Walk the canals, climb the Belfry if the queue allows, and accept that you'll share the lanes with other visitors — Bruges earns its reputation anyway. Head back to Ghent or straight to Brussels airport depending on your flight.

<Info>
  Bruges is gorgeous but crowded. If you've seen it before, swap day eight for a
  second day in Ghent or an Antwerp return with time at the Rubenshuis.
</Info>

## Travel tips

This route assumes eight nights and seven train changes between bases — all straightforward on NS and SNCB. Intercity trains run frequently; you rarely need seat reservations on domestic legs.

Pack light. Amsterdam Centraal, Rotterdam Centraal, and Gent-Sint-Pieters are all easy with a rolling bag, but cobblestones in Delft, Ghent, and Bruges will remind you why a backpack beats a suitcase.

Book somewhere in Ghent inside or near the historic center. The canal belt is worth the premium.

<Info>
  Keukenhof is seasonal — check opening dates before you book day four. Outside
  mid-March to mid-May, replace it with Leiden, The Hague, or an extra day in
  Amsterdam.
</Info>
