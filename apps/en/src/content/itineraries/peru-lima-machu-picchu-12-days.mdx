## Days 1–2: The coast

Peru doesn't ask you to choose between desert and ocean — on the first two days you get both, plus a night in one of the continent's great food cities.

### Paracas and Huacachina

Start south of Lima in **Paracas**, a small fishing town on a natural reserve where the desert meets the Pacific. The classic half-day outing is a boat trip to the **Ballestas Islands** — rocky outcrops crowded with sea lions, Humboldt penguins, and seabirds. It's often called the "poor man's Galápagos," and while that's a stretch, the wildlife density for a two-hour ride is genuinely impressive.

<AssetFigure
  path="peru/paracas.JPG"
  alt="Paracas coastline with desert hills meeting the Pacific"
  caption="Paracas — where the desert meets the ocean"
/>

From Paracas, it's a short hop inland to **Huacachina**, a palm-fringed lagoon surrounded by towering sand dunes. This is one of the few true desert oases in South America, and the main activity is gloriously simple: strap on a sandboard, ride a dune buggy up the ridges, and slide back down. Sunset from the top of the dunes, with the oasis glowing below, is worth the sand in your shoes.

<AssetFigure

  caption="Huacachina oasis at sunset"
/>

### Lima

Day two is **Lima** — and Lima is really about eating. The capital has become a global dining destination, with cevicherías, picanterías, and world-ranked restaurants across Miraflores and Barranco. Walk the malecón above the cliffs, explore the bohemian streets of Barranco, and save your appetite for dinner.

<AssetFigure
  path="peru/lima.JPG"
  alt="Lima coastline seen from the Miraflores cliffs"
  caption="The Miraflores malecón above the Pacific"
/>

**Recommended activities:**

- Ceviche lunch at a classic spot like **La Mar** or **Pescados Capiales**
- Walk through **Barranco** — street art, the Puente de los Suspiros, and cafés
- **Miraflores** cliff walk at golden hour

## Days 3–6: Cusco and the Sacred Valley

### Flying to Cusco

On day three, fly from Lima to **Cusco** (about 1.5 hours). At 3,400 m, the altitude hits immediately — take the first day easy. Walk the Plaza de Armas, sip coca tea, and resist the urge to climb every staircase you see. Your body will thank you when the trek starts.

### Sacred Valley: Pisac and Ollantaytambo

Day four is a full **Sacred Valley** loop. Start at **Pisac**, where a huge Inca ruin complex sits on a ridge above a colourful Sunday market — textiles, ceramics, and produce spread across the plaza. Then continue deeper into the valley to **Ollantaytambo**, a living Inca town where massive terraced fortifications still guard the valley entrance. The scale of the stonework here is a good preview of what's coming at Machu Picchu.

### Moray and Maras

Day five heads to two of the valley's most unusual sites. **Moray** is a series of concentric circular terraces carved into a natural depression — thought to have been an Inca agricultural laboratory, testing crops at different microclimates. Nearby **Maras** is something entirely different: thousands of salt pans cascading down a hillside, still harvested by local families the same way they have been for centuries. The white pools against the brown hills make for an extraordinary sight.

### Inti Raymi

If you time the trip for late June, day six coincides with **Inti Raymi** — the Festival of the Sun. Cusco fills with processions, dancers in gold and crimson, and re-enactments of Inca ceremonies on the winter solstice. The whole city becomes a stage. We were lucky enough to catch it during our 2022 visit — see our [Festivals in Cusco](/spotlights/festivals-in-cusco) spotlight for more.

<Info type="tip">
  Inti Raymi falls on June 24 each year. Book accommodation in Cusco months
  ahead — the city fills up fast.
</Info>

## Days 7–10: Salkantay trek to Machu Picchu

The second half of the trip is the **Salkantay trek** — four days from high-altitude camps through cloud forest to Machu Picchu. It's an alternative to the oversubscribed Inca Trail, and in our experience one of the best multi-day hikes in South America.

We went with a guided tour from [Salkantay Trekking](https://www.salkantaytrekking.com/) and wrote up the full experience in our [Salkantay trek story](/stories/salkantay-trek-machu-picchu). Here's how the days break down:

**Day 7** — Humantay Lake and camp at Soraypampa (3,800 m). A short acclimatization hike to a stunning blue glacial lagoon.

## Days 11–12: Homeward

### Cusco

Day eleven is a buffer in **Cusco** — wander San Pedro market, revisit a favourite café, buy last-minute alpaca wool gifts. After four days of trekking, your legs will appreciate the flat(ter) streets.

### Lima

Fly back to **Lima** on day twelve for a final night and the international flight home. **Barranco** is the neighbourhood to linger in — colourful streets, street art, and one last round of ceviche before the long flight.

## Travel tips

Peru rewards slow travel at altitude. Don't rush acclimatization in Cusco — the Sacred Valley days before the trek are partly sightseeing, partly letting your body adjust.

Domestic flights between Lima and Cusco are frequent and relatively cheap with carriers like LATAM and Sky Airline. Book early for June (Inti Raymi season).

The Salkantay trek can be done independently, but a guided tour with porters and a cook makes the high-altitude days much more enjoyable. Whichever you choose, pack layers — you'll go from near-freezing at the pass to humid jungle within 48 hours.

<Info type="warning">
  Machu Picchu tickets are limited and sell out weeks ahead. Coordinate your
  trek end date with your entry slot before booking anything else.
</Info>
