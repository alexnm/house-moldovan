# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary readers:** curious travelers planning trips or revisiting places they have been. They arrive from search, social links, or RSS with a specific destination or story in mind, or they browse the journal and explore pages to discover where to go next.

**Context:** evening reading on a phone or laptop, often with photos as important as the prose. They want honest field notes, not brochure copy. Some readers only want photo highlights (spotlights); others want day-by-day plans (itineraries) or narrative guides (stories).

**Sister brand:** Romanian Carpathian hiking lives on a separate site (working name *Pe creastă*, domain `pecreasta.ro`). Same authors, different audience and product contract. Cross-promo from this journal is optional and currently commented out in nav/footer/about; when enabled, link to `https://pecreasta.ro`, never a `/ro` subpath.

## Product Purpose

House Moldovan is a photographic travel journal by Mela and Alex (`housemoldovan.com`). It organizes personal travel writing across four regions (South America, Asia, Middle East, Europe) and three note types: stories, spotlights, and itineraries.

Success looks like: a reader finds a useful, memorable piece about a place, understands where it sits geographically, and trusts the photography and voice enough to return or subscribe via RSS. The site is static, fast, and content-driven; the design should get out of the way of the images and writing.

## Brand Personality

**Three words:** warm, photographic, grounded.

**Voice:** first-person, conversational, fellow-traveler energy ("Hi there fellow travelers!"). Practical when it matters (months, routes, tips), never corporate or influencer-performative.

**Emotional goals:** wanderlust with substance; the feeling of flipping through a well-edited travel magazine on a dark evening, not scrolling a template blog. Region color and cinematic motion should feel intentional, not decorative.

**References (directional, not copies):** Condé Nast Traveler photo essays for photography-first pacing; personal travel blogs with strong author voice; region-as-chapter organization like a well-made atlas you actually read.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI travel-blog templates: identical card grids, frosted-glass nav, stock-placeholder heroes, interchangeable Fraunces-and-gradient aesthetics.
- Brochure tourism sites: superlatives, "hidden gem" filler, no author presence.
- Influencer layouts: engagement bait, metric heroes, modal newsletter popups.
- Magazine cosplay without photography: big display type and drop caps where the images should lead.
- Treating this journal and the Romanian hiking sister site as one visual product (they share infrastructure, not identity).

## Design Principles

1. **Photography is the headline.** Layout, type, and motion frame the images; they do not compete with them.
2. **Place-first wayfinding.** Region → country → note is the mental model. Navigation and explore surfaces should reinforce geography, not arbitrary categories.
3. **Personal, not promotional.** Write like travelers sharing what they saw, not a brand selling destinations.
4. **Committed regional identity.** Each region earns its own color and mood; the system should feel like four chapters of one journal, not one neutral skin.
5. **Craft over template.** Scroll rhythm, asymmetric grids, and restrained motion are welcome when they serve reading; default patterns (card grids, glass chrome, side-stripe accents) are not.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.1 AA** (contrast, keyboard access, alt text on informative images, focus visibility).
- Honor **`prefers-reduced-motion`** for parallax and scroll-driven reveals (already in CSS; maintain this).
- Informative photos in stories and spotlights need descriptive alt text; decorative images may be empty.
