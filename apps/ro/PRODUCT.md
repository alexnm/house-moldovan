# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary readers:** Romanian weekend and day hikers comparing Carpathian routes at home, on a phone or laptop, deciding whether a trail is the right one for tomorrow. They care about honest difficulty, duration, distance, elevation gain, marcaje, and a trustworthy photo of the terrain, not tourism copy.

**Context:** kitchen-table planning (and sometimes a last look before packing). Some readers want a GPX download and map; others want a clear "is this for me?" signal from stats plus field notes.

**Authors:** Mela and Alex. Same people as House Moldovan; this site speaks to a different job and language.

**Sister brand:** the English photographic travel journal at `housemoldovan.com`. Linked from the footer and the home about section; not the primary product here.

## Product Purpose

*Pe creste* is a personal Romanian hiking journal for routes in the Carpathians (`pecreasta.ro`). It organizes hikes by massif (Făgăraș, Retezat, Apuseni, Rodnei, Parâng, Piatra Craiului) with elevation profiles, GPX tracks, waymarks, and notes for the days when details matter.

Success looks like: a reader picks a hike with confidence from photos and stats, understands which massif it belongs to, and can take the GPX (and field notes) onto the trail. The site is static, fast, and decision-oriented; design should make comparison easy without becoming a dashboard.

## Brand Personality

**Three words:** practic, onest, de teren.

**Voice:** personal journal in Romanian, anti-tourism. Details over brochure language ("note pentru zilele când contează detaliile, nu textul de turism"). Conversational and grounded; first-person field notes welcome; avoid impersonal guidebook prose.

**Emotional goals:** quiet confidence before a hike; the feeling of a paper marcaj and a pencil note on the kitchen table, not an app gamifying peaks.

**Creative north star (visual):** "Marcaj pe hârtie." Photography sells the idea; difficulty, duration, distance, and gain close the decision.

**References (directional, not copies):** well-made Romanian field notes and cartography; kitchen-table planning surfaces; trail-sign typography over lifestyle magazine cosplay.

## Anti-references

- AllTrails-style dashboards: dense metric chrome, leaderboard energy, map-app UI dressed as a journal.
- Impersonal trail descriptions: guidebook boilerplate, no author, no lived texture.
- Brochure tourism portals: superlatives, "hidden gem" filler, destination marketing.
- Treating this site and House Moldovan as one visual product (shared foundation, separate identity).
- Influencer hike-reel layouts: engagement bait, metric heroes, modal spam.

## Design Principles

1. **Massif → trail is the mental model.** Atlas chapters are the mountain ranges; individual hikes live inside a massif. Wayfinding should reinforce geography first.
2. **Stats close the decision; photos sell the idea.** Difficulty, duration, distance, and gain must be scannable on cards and hike pages. Photography frames desire; numbers earn trust.
3. **Difficulty is a compare signal, not the chapter system.** Jade / saffron / terracotta on hike cards help rank effort at a glance. Do not organize the site around difficulty the way House Moldovan organizes around world regions.
4. **GPX, elevation, and marcaje are first-class.** Map, profile, download, and waymark badges are product, not decoration. Waymark colors stay data-bound; never reuse them as UI chrome accents.
5. **Personal field notes, not tourism copy.** Write like hikers who were there. Craft over template; reject dashboard density and brochure filler.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.1 AA** (contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, Romanian labels on waymark badges).
- Honor **`prefers-reduced-motion`** for parallax and scroll-driven reveals.
- Informative hike and range photos need descriptive alt text; decorative images may be empty.
- Difficulty and season labels must remain readable without relying on color alone (text kickers accompany accent ticks).
